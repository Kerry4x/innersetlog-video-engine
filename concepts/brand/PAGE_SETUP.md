## 4. Narration voice
**What it has to do:** carry a story with weight but never preach; sound like someone telling you something true, close to the mic.
**Pace:** the clay reference reads at 197–218 words/min; for scripture lines we slow to ~150–170 and keep the modern section near ~190.

**ElevenLabs Voice Design prompts to try** (describe the voice; generate a few, keep the best):
1. *Warm older storyteller:* "Middle-aged to older male narrator, warm and low, calm and unhurried, slight gravel, speaks close to the microphone like he is telling a story by a fire, sincere, never theatrical, neutral accent, clean studio recording."
2. *Grounded younger voice:* "Male narrator in his thirties, clear and grounded, conversational but serious, steady pace, a little intimate, neutral accent, no announcer tone, clean studio recording."
3. *Quiet authority (female):* "Female narrator in her forties, calm, warm, low-pitched, confident and quiet, measured pacing, storytelling tone, neutral accent, clean studio recording."

**Audition line** (use the same text for every candidate so they compare fairly):
> "Around 1000 BC, a prophet went to the most powerful king in Israel with a story about a stolen lamb. By the time the story was over, the king had passed judgment on himself without knowing it. 'Thou art the man.'"

Pick on: does the KJV line sound natural, not costume-like; does the hook pull you in; would you listen for ten minutes.
