# World reference: Jerusalem around 1000 BC (David's city)

Purpose: make the miniature world and the characters *make sense* for the period. Not a copy of any film or illustration; a fact base we design from.
Labels: **[checked]** = confirmed in a source this session · **[text]** = what the KJV says (reference given) · **[recalled]** = from memory, verify before stating on screen · **[staging]** = our choice where nothing is known.

## The city
- **Small.** David's Jerusalem is the "City of David" on a narrow ridge south of the later Temple Mount, with steep valleys on both sides (Kidron to the east). **[recalled: ridge size, roughly 4–6 ha / 10–15 acres]**
- "David took the strong hold of Zion: the same is the city of David." **[text: 2 Sam 5:7]**
- **Palace at the north top of the ridge.** Eilat Mazar excavated the *Large Stone Structure* and argued it was David's palace (11th–10th c. BC); Finkelstein disputes the dating; Na'aman accepts it as a good candidate. **[checked]** We show a large stone building there **[staging, consistent with Mazar]**.
- **The Stepped Stone Structure** (a huge stepped stone mantle on the east slope) supports the Large Stone Structure; it may be the *Millo* of the text. **[checked]** "David built round about from Millo and inward." **[text: 2 Sam 5:9]**
- **Cedar and Phoenician builders:** "Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, and cedar trees, and carpenters, and masons: and they built David an house." **[text: 2 Sam 5:11]** → the palace gets cedar beams and dressed-stone corners **[staging]**.
- **The palace roof was walkable:** David "walked upon the roof of the king's house." **[text: 2 Sam 11:2]**
- **No Temple yet** (that comes under Solomon). Don't show one.

## Houses (what fills the city)
- Typical Iron Age house: **four-room house**. Entered from an open courtyard, rooms reached from the central space, often a **second story** reached by ladder or stairs; ground floor for livestock and storage. **[checked]**
- **Flat roofs:** wooden beams across the walls, rafters, brush on top, then mud/clay/plaster. Roofs used for drying food and storage. **[checked]**
- **Roof parapets:** "thou shalt make a battlement for thy roof." **[text: Deut 22:8]** (archaeological confirmation not found in this search)
- Walls of rough fieldstone, plastered. On a slope, houses step down **terraces**. **[recalled]**

## People and clothes
- Almost no textiles survive; knowledge comes from figurines, ivories and foreign reliefs (e.g. the Assyrian Lachish reliefs, 701 BC, three centuries later). **[checked]**
- Working assumption: men in a **knee-to-ankle tunic** with a belt and a **wool mantle** over it; beards; simple head cloths. Undyed wool browns, creams and greys for ordinary people; color is expensive. **[recalled; verify against King & Stager, *Life in Biblical Israel*, clothing chapter]**
- Royal color: purple was a royal luxury in the region ("purple raiment that was on the kings of Midian"). **[text: Judges 8:26]**

## Thrones and royal imagery
- The **Megiddo ivory** (about 1180 BC) shows a king on a **sphinx-armed throne**, holding a cup, with a musician playing a lyre before him. **[checked]**
- Solomon's later throne had **lions** beside the armrests and on the steps. **[text: 1 Kings 10:19–20]**
→ David's throne: wood with **lion/sphinx armrests**, cushions, a footstool. **[staging, from regional royal imagery]**
- David is also a musician (the lyre/*kinnor*); a lyre in the room is a fair character detail. **[text: 1 Sam 16:23]**

## The scene itself (2 Samuel 12) and how it must be acted
- Where they met is **not stated**: "the LORD sent Nathan unto David. And he came unto him." **[text: 2 Sam 12:1]** The throne room is **[staging]**.
- Nathan **tells a story** (vv1–4): the rich man with "exceeding many flocks and herds", the poor man with "one little ewe lamb", which "lay in his bosom, and was unto him as a daughter." **[text]** → Nathan is *active*: a storyteller who shapes the lamb with his hands, draws the king in.
- David **gets angry, not shocked**: "David's anger was greatly kindled against the man" and he passes sentence ("the man that hath done this thing shall surely die") and orders the lamb restored "fourfold." **[text: vv5–6]**
- Nathan's line is **short and steady**: "Thou art the man." **[text: v7]** Then he delivers God's message at length (vv7–12).
- David's answer is a collapse, not a scream: "I have sinned against the LORD." **[text: v13]**

**Acting rule:** the stillest person holds the power. Nathan gets quieter and stiller as the scene tightens; David moves from comfort → leaning in → anger (leaning forward, fist, pointing) → frozen → slowly sinking, eyes down.

## Visual reference sources to study (look, don't copy)
- Excavation photos and reconstructions of the City of David (Large Stone Structure, Stepped Stone Structure).
- Iron Age house reconstructions (four-room house drawings; models at museums such as the Israel Museum).
- The Megiddo ivories (throne, dress, lyre); the Lachish reliefs at the British Museum (Judahite dress, 701 BC).
- Study-Bible illustrations (e.g. the drawings in study Bibles like the ESV Study Bible) are **artists' reconstructions**: good for mood and plausibility checks, not as sources of fact, and not to copy.
