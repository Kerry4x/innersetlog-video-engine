# Short 03 · "Jesus called out using God as an excuse" (Corban, Mark 7)

**Lane:** what the Bible actually says vs religion (the most direct statement of the thesis) · **Structure:** story → "you see" turn → a modern situation → the text → a closing line
**Length:** ~165 words ≈ 60 s · **Status:** draft 1 · **Thesis angle:** man-made tradition used to dodge what God actually commanded

## Title options
1. Jesus Called Out Religious Leaders for Using God as an Excuse
2. The Religious Loophole Jesus Hated
3. When "I Gave It to God" Was a Lie

## Script

| # | Beat | Narration | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | Jesus once accused the religious leaders of his day of using God as an excuse to abandon their own parents. | Robed leaders in a crowd; Jesus facing them |
| 2 | Story | There was a practice called Corban. You could declare your money "a gift to God"… and then you didn't have to use it to help your aging father and mother. | Coins placed in a sealed box; an old couple at an empty table |
| 3 | Story | It looked holy. It sounded devout. | Heads nodding, hands raised |
| 4 | **The text** | Jesus said: *"Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition."* God had said honor your father and mother. They found a religious way around it. | Close on Jesus; then the old couple again |
| 5 | **Turn** | You see… religion can be the best place to hide from what God actually asked you to do. | Hard cut to today: a church parking lot |
| 6 | Modern | "I'll pray for you" instead of showing up. At church three nights a week, and the call home never happens. Serving everyone at the event, and ignoring the person at your own table. | Quick cuts: a text saying "praying for you"; a missed call from Mom; an empty seat at home |
| 7 | **Close line** | Jesus didn't call that devotion. He said it made the word of God of no effect. | Hold; black |

## Accuracy notes (KJV)
- **Mark 7:9–13** (parallel: Matthew 15:3–6). The confrontation is with "the Pharisees, and certain of the scribes" (Mark 7:1, 5), hence "religious leaders" rather than "the most religious men".
- 7:9: "Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition."
- 7:10: Jesus cites Moses: "Honour thy father and thy mother."
- 7:11: "Corban, that is to say, a gift": declaring something devoted to God so it couldn't be used to support one's parents (7:12).
- 7:13: "Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition." The close line paraphrases this.
- Historical nuance for long-form: how Corban vows worked in practice is discussed by scholars. The short keeps to what the text itself says.

## Tone guardrails
- The critique is Jesus' own words aimed at a practice, not the narrator attacking people. The modern examples are behaviors anyone could recognize in themselves, not "Christians do this".
