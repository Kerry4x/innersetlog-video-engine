## Accuracy notes (verified against the text, KJV, public domain)
- **Amos was a farmer, not a professional prophet:** "I was no prophet, neither was I a prophet's son; but I was an herdman, and a gatherer of sycomore fruit" (Amos 7:14). Sycomore = a kind of fig.
- **Bethel was the royal sanctuary:** the priest Amaziah calls it "the king's chapel, and… the king's court" and tells Amos to go back to Judah (Amos 7:10–13).
- **Timing:** Amos prophesied in the days of Jeroboam II of Israel (Amos 1:1), commonly dated to about 760–750 BC. "Around 760 BC" is the standard approximation.
- **The quote:** Amos 5:21 ("I hate, I despise your feast days…") and 5:23 ("Take thou away from me the noise of thy songs…"), joined with an ellipsis.
- **The why:** bribes and turning aside the poor "in the gate" (= the court) (Amos 5:12); selling "the poor for a pair of shoes" (Amos 2:6).
- **The closing verse:** Amos 5:24.
- **One simplification to be aware of:** the script places the 5:21–23 message in the Bethel scene. The book collects Amos's messages to Israel, which were aimed at its worship at Bethel and Gilgal (5:5), and chapter 7 places him at Bethel. The compression is fair for a short, but the long-form version should say "messages like this".

## Tone guardrails
- Aim the critique where the text aims it: at worship without justice, **not at Christians as a group**. The script says "the people who sang the loudest and lived the least like it", not "Christians".
- No mocking. The story does the convicting, the narrator doesn't.
- The turn invites the viewer back to the text, which is the channel's thesis: *the word, not the religion around it.*
