# Working concept: same human, new package

## The thesis (Kerry's)
The Bible isn't a storybook; it is the living word of God and has always applied to humans. People treat it as old stories and overlook what it reveals: the **mechanisms** behind human behavior. Human nature hasn't changed in thousands of years. The *packages* our problems come in keep changing (technology, culture, a new study with a new name), but what causes them, and what's behind them, is the same.

*Wording note:* say "human nature hasn't changed" / "same problems, new package", not "humans haven't evolved" (that invites biology objections and isn't the claim).

## Two directions, one thesis
Either direction works; what matters is that the **mechanism** connects then and now.

**A. Story-first (Bible → now)**: the default for **shorts** (like the Clayrified parable → "you" structure):
1. **The story:** a Bible moment, told accurately and in context.
2. **The mechanism:** what that moment reveals about people.
3. **Now:** the same mechanism in a situation people live today (one connected situation, not a list).
4. **What it reveals:** the point the story makes about us. Not forced into advice.

**B. Package-first (now → Bible)**: works well for **long-form** and for modern hooks:
1. **The package:** something modern people recognize (a psychology finding, a habit, a cultural moment).
2. **The mechanism:** what's actually driving it, stated precisely.
3. **The original:** where scripture shows the same mechanism.
4. **What it reveals:** what the modern package misses and the text sees.

Long-form can go deeper in either direction. Differences between the modern package and the biblical account are **part of the point** (different package, same mechanism), so both must be stated exactly.

## Script standards (Kerry, 2026-11-16)
- Judge a script by whether it makes sense and captures the whole idea, not by voice or runtime.
- No filler, no repeating what was already said, no cutting to hit a length if it leaves fragments.
- Nothing misused: claims about the text and about studies must be accurate (label anything unverified).
- Length is whatever the complete idea needs.

## Hook pattern (from Kerry's pick, short 01)
Open with **a date + a specific, unexpected person or detail + a high-stakes place or person + an action**, not a summary. Example: "Around 760 BC, a fig farmer walked into the king's own temple…"
- The date signals *this really happened*.
- The unexpected person creates tension against the high-stakes setting.
- It must stay accurate: only stage what the text actually says (the Amos hook overreached here; David's version uses "went to" because the text says Nathan "came unto him").
- Open the question; don't give away how it resolves.

## Production approach
- Research and write the **long-form** version of an idea first (room for the full mechanism).
- Cut **shorts that each carry one complete idea**, start to finish, not trimmed fragments of the long one.
