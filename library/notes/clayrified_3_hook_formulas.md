# Clayrified free lesson: "The 3 Hook Formulas" (summary + our adaptation)

Source: PDF Kerry received from @clayrified (3 pages, Nov 2026). Summarized, not copied. Their claims: 40M views on one video, 170M+ total, 450K+ followers; they state results are not typical.

## Their three formulas
| # | Formula | Shape | Their example (views) | Rules they give |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unfair contrast** | Someone does a small thing; someone else reacts in a way that makes no sense. Two short sentences, the second flips the first. | Two monks and the woman in the mud (39.7M); the man who spat at the Buddha (4.8M) | Both sentences under ~10 words; stronger, stranger sentence second; explain nothing yet |
| 2 | **Impossible task** | Someone with power asks for something that sounds impossible; say the rule out loud. | The chalk line (11.3M); the ring (4.4M) | Rule clear in one sentence; the answer must feel obvious once revealed, never a trick |
| 3 | **Hidden truth** | Show what everyone believed, then hint the truth underneath is the opposite. | The clay-covered golden Buddha (5.1M); armoring the wrong part of the plane (1.4M+) | Name the thing everyone could see; promise what's hidden, don't reveal it |

**One rule for all three:** start with the best line. No warm-up, no slow shot of the setting; if the best line is mid-script, move it to the start.

## How we use them (Kerry, 2026-11-16)
- **Reference only.** These stay in the library and come out only when a story already has that shape. Never bend a story to fit one, and never pick a formula first and then look for a story.
- They sit inside our hook standard (concrete, opens the question, accurate). The flip, task or hidden truth has to be in the text itself.
- **Keep from it:** start with the best line. Our David/Nathan look tests broke this by opening on a slow aerial of the city with "Around 1000 BC". The opening shot should be the most charged moment.

## Fit check: an example of forcing it (rejected)
- *Impossible task* on Solomon's judgment (1 Kings 3): doesn't fit. Nobody with power sets an impossible rule for someone else; the king himself is solving a dispute.
- *Hidden truth* on Amos 5: doesn't fit. Amos is an open rebuke, not something everyone believed that hides an opposite underneath.
- *Unfair contrast* on David and Nathan (2 Sam 12): "A prophet told the king a story. The king sentenced himself to death." **Usable but slightly forced.** It isn't really "a small act plus a reaction that makes no sense"; it's a self-judgment twist. And "sentenced himself" compresses the text: David condemned "the man that hath done this thing" (v5) without knowing it was him, and he was spared (v13).
