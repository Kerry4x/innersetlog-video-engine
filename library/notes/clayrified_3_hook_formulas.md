# Clayrified free lesson: "The 3 Hook Formulas" (summary + our adaptation)

Source: PDF Kerry received from @clayrified (3 pages, Nov 2026). Summarized, not copied. Their claims: 40M views on one video, 170M+ total, 450K+ followers; they state results are not typical.

## Their three formulas
| # | Formula | Shape | Their example (views) | Rules they give |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unfair contrast** | Someone does a small thing; someone else reacts in a way that makes no sense. Two short sentences, the second flips the first. | Two monks and the woman in the mud (39.7M); the man who spat at the Buddha (4.8M) | Both sentences under ~10 words; stronger, stranger sentence second; explain nothing yet |
| 2 | **Impossible task** | Someone with power asks for something that sounds impossible; say the rule out loud. | The chalk line (11.3M); the ring (4.4M) | Rule clear in one sentence; the answer must feel obvious once revealed, never a trick |
| 3 | **Hidden truth** | Show what everyone believed, then hint the truth underneath is the opposite. | The clay-covered golden Buddha (5.1M); armoring the wrong part of the plane (1.4M+) | Name the thing everyone could see; promise what's hidden, don't reveal it |

**One rule for all three:** start with the best line. No warm-up, no slow shot of the setting; if the best line is mid-script, move it to the start.

## What it means for us
- These are **three tools, not one template.** That matches Kerry's rule (no repeated hook style); rotate them and still let each story's strongest moment decide.
- They sit inside our existing hook standard (concrete, opens the question, accurate). Extra caution for us: the "flip" must come from the text, never from bending it.
- **Our David/Nathan look tests broke the main rule.** They opened on a slow aerial of the city with "Around 1000 BC". The opening shot should be the most charged moment, with the setting shown later.

## Scripture drafts in each formula (verses recalled; check before production)
- **Unfair contrast (2 Sam 12):** "A prophet told the king a story. The king sentenced himself to death." (David's verdict, v5; he is spared, v13.)
- **Unfair contrast (Mark 7:9–13):** "He gave his money to God. Jesus said he was rejecting God's command." (Corban.)
- **Impossible task (1 Kings 3:16–28):** "Two mothers. One baby. No witnesses. The king called for a sword." (v18: no one else was in the house.)
- **Hidden truth (Amos 5:21–24):** "They were the most religious nation on earth. God said: take away the noise of thy songs." (v23)
