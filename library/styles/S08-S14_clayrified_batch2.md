## 0. The seven scripts

Paraphrased; short exact quotes only.

**The formula, now confirmed across 10 videos:**
1. **Macro sensory opener:** an extreme close-up of a physical action (needle, seed, wine, nail), narrated in present tense. The story's central object is in shot 1.
2. **Story** in present tense, fast, with one line of dialogue at the turn.
3. **Pivot:** "In life, …" (S09, S12–S14) or "You see, in life, …" (S10). S11 skips it.
4. **Three modern examples**, each one sentence and very specific (a boss's "can we talk tomorrow?" text, the LinkedIn "expert", googling a pain at 11pm).
5. **Close:** a short imperative that **calls back the story's object** ("read the ring", "pick up the chalk", "carry it in"). From S11 on, a follow CTA is added.

**Sources are public-domain parables and folk stories** (Solomon/Persian ring legend, Akbar–Birbal, Chinese idiom 杯弓蛇影, a Vasari anecdote, a folk carpenter tale). Nothing is invented; the value is in the retelling and the modern application.

- **Motion is on 2s** (each pose held two frames = 12 poses/s), with few runs on 3s or longer. That is the stop-motion signature, kept even though the footage is very likely generated (see §5).
- **The camera does not move within a shot:** zero zoom or pan segments ≥ 0.4 s in all seven. Every change of framing is a cut.
- **Shot grammar:** mostly extreme close-ups (eyes, hands, the object) intercut with a few wide establishing shots. Emotion comes from **eyelids** (heavy, half-closed by default), a sweat bead, a color change (the sick man turns grey in S13).

## 3. Look

- **Photoreal plasticine:** fingerprints and tool marks, fabric weave on clothes, real shallow depth of field, practical light (lamps, windows, candles).
- **Character design:** big noses, white eyes with small pupils, **heavy upper lids**, thick worm-like brows, simple hands. Very consistent across all ten videos; this design is their brand.
- **Two worlds:** the story is warm, low-key and amber (near-black 15–26% of pixels in the story-heavy videos); the modern section turns bright and flat (offices, pastel kitchens, phones). S10's modern palette has only 5% near-black.
- **Palette anchors:** clay skin `#BD6D36`–`#E09D63`, dark wood `#2A1210`–`#4C2A1D`, night blue `#15202F`, a single strong accent per story (red `#B90D24` in S12, gold in S09).
- **Captions:** unchanged from batch 1: bold yellow serif, 2–5 words, lower-middle.

## 5. How it is most likely made (evidence, not confirmed)

Strong signs the footage is **AI image/video generation** styled as clay, not hand-built stop-motion:
- **Gibberish text:** the video-editor screen in S08 has unreadable menu labels; the height chart in S10 reads "2m, 5m, 3m, 4m, 3m, 6m".
- **Prompt drift:** S10's closing shot draws a chalk spiral while the narration is about drawing a longer line.
- **Production volume and fidelity:** studio-grade sets and new characters in every video, at a pace a hand-animated team couldn't keep up.
- **S08 is their course ad** ("We analyzed 6,000 videos…", the two-monks short "hit 10 million views in a single day"; course modules: Storytelling, Production, Monetization).

The on-2s timing, the locked camera and the 1.5–2 s shots all fit short generated clips trimmed to one caption phrase each.

## 6. What this means for us

- Their moat is **not** the look (anyone with the same tools gets close); it is the **writing formula** plus consistent characters.
- General craft worth reusing (not specific to them): macro object opener, story object = closing callback, three hyper-specific modern examples, on-2s motion, locked camera per shot, eyelids as the main acting tool.
- **Don't copy:** their character design (big nose, heavy lids, worm brows), the yellow serif captions, the "In life," pivot wording, or their parables.
