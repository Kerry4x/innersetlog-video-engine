## 0. Script breakdown

The traditional Zen story of two monks and a woman, retold, then applied to the viewer. Paraphrased; short exact quotes only.

| Time | Beat | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | **Hook: in medias res** | "Two monks are walking home…": a road turned to deep mud. Opens on an extreme close-up of a sandal sinking into mud. |
| 0:05–0:19 | The act | The older monk carries a stranded woman across, sets her down, and keeps walking. |
| 0:19–0:34 | Silent resentment | The younger monk's jaw tightens: past fields, through the village, up the hill. A **rule of three** in the walk montage. |
| 0:34–0:45 | Outburst | "We have taken a vow…" |
| 0:45–0:53 | **The line** | The older monk: he set her down that morning. "Why are you still carrying her?" |
| 0:53–1:01 | **Pivot to the viewer** | "You see, in life, the thing that hurts you often takes only four seconds to happen." The world switches to **modern clay scenes** (wall clock, a couple at a kitchen table). |
| 1:01–1:14 | Application montage | Three modern examples: a sharp remark at dinner, a cold look, a text ending "with a harsh full stop". Then "you carry it home". |
| 1:14–1:17 | Command | "Put it down exactly where it happened." |

Structure: **parable (ancient world) → "You see, in life…" → modern-day examples in the same clay world → one imperative line.**

## 2. Technique

- **40% hold · 52% local motion** · 2% zoom · 1% pan · 5% cut. No camera moves ≥ 0.4 s.
- **Reaction-shot ping-pong** in the modern section: her face, his face, a hand twisting a sleeve, a mug, a phone. Emotion is carried by alternating close-ups, not by dialogue.

## 5. What makes it work

1. **Two worlds, one style:** the parable is "then", the application is "now", and the change in color and setting signals the pivot without any title card.
2. **Specific, tiny, relatable examples** ("a text message ending with a harsh full stop") land the abstract lesson.
3. **The pivot phrase "You see, …"** (also used as the line-2 opener in the T2 niche) is a reusable hinge.
