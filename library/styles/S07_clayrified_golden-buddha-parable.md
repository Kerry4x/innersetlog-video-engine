## 0. Script breakdown

Retells the well-known story of the Golden Buddha (a clay-covered statue found to be solid gold), then turns it into a lesson about the viewer. Paraphrased; short exact quotes only.

| Time | Beat | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:07 | **Hook: mystery** | "For 200 years, a plain clay Buddha sat quietly in a temple." It was heavier than it should be; nobody cared. |
| 0:07–0:18 | Inciting event | Moving it, a rope slips, a crack opens, and it rains overnight. |
| 0:18–0:33 | **Reveal** | A monk's flashlight catches a gleam; they chip away the clay. "Underneath, the Buddha was solid gold." |
| 0:33–0:47 | Backstory | Monks disguised it in clay before an invading army came; they were killed and the secret was lost. |
| 0:47–0:50 | **Pivot to the viewer** | "At some point in your life, you did the exact same thing." |
| 0:50–1:08 | Metaphor made literal | Rejection made you "cover your gold in clay". Visualized as a **golden clay man in a red scarf hiding a small blue bird** among plain grey clay people. |
| 1:08–1:13 | Command | Nothing was taken, only covered. "Go find the crack." |

Structure: **mystery object → reveal → why it was hidden → "you did the same thing" → the metaphor becomes a character → a 4-word imperative.**
